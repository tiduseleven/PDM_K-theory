\subsection{Simplicial objects and geometric realisations}
Simplices prominently arise in many aspects of algebraic topology, such as simplicial complexes to singular (co-)homology. They may be regarded all together as a category which gives rise to interesting geometric structures.

\begin{definition}
    The \emph{simplex category} $\Delta$ consists of objects given by sets $\textbf{n}$ and non-strictly order-preserving maps.
    The maps of $\Delta$ are generated by \emph{coface maps} 
    \[ \delta_i : j \in \textbf{n} \mapsto 
    \begin{cases}
        j \in \textbf{n+1} & \text{if } j < i \\
        j + 1 \in \textbf{n+1} & \text{if } j \geq i
    \end{cases}
    \]
    with $0 \leq j \leq n$ and \emph{codegeneracy maps}
    \[
        \sigma_i : j \in \textbf{n+1} \mapsto \begin{cases}
            j \in \textbf{n} & \text{if } j \leq i \\
            j - 1 \in \textbf{n} & \text{if } j > i
        \end{cases}
    \]
    with $0 \leq j \leq n$.
\end{definition}

The reason for naming the generators coface and codegeneracy maps is that they will represent face and degeneracy maps between geometric simplices, i.e. projecting an $(n+1)$-simplex onto one of its faces and embedding an $n$-simplex as the face of an $(n+1)$-simplex.

\begin{definition}
    Let $\CC$ be a category. A \emph{simplicial object} in $\CC$ is a functor $S: \op{\Delta} \to \CC$. We denote $C_n$ for the object $S(\textbf{n})$.

    The images of codegeneracy maps are called \emph{degeneracy maps} and the images of coface maps are called \emph{face maps}.


    A \emph{simplicial set} is a simplicial object in the category of sets. The category of simplicial sets with natural transformations between them is denoted $\sSet$. 
    
    A \emph{simplicial space} is a simplicial object in the category of topological spaces.
\end{definition}

Since all morphisms in $\Delta$ are generated by coface and codegeneracies maps, it is sufficient to define the functor on the codegeneracy and coface maps. That means, for a given codegeneracy $\delta_i : \textbf{n+1} \to \textbf{n}$, to define a map $d_i: C_{n+1} \to C_n$ and, for a given coface map $\sigma_i : \textbf{n} \to \textbf{n+1}$, to define a map $s_i : C_{n+1} \to C_{n}$. One must then check functoriality on the defined maps.

The simplest example of a simplicial set comes naturally from the Hom-sets of the simplex category.

\begin{example}[The standard $n$-simplex]
    The standard $n$-simplex $\Delta^n$ is the simplicial set defined as the functor
    \[ \Hom[\Delta]{-}{\textbf{n}} : \op{\Delta} \to \Set. \]
\end{example}

Every category gives rise to a simplicial set by chains of composable morphisms.

\begin{definition}
    Let $\CC$ be a small category. The \emph{nerve} of the category $\CC$ is the simplicial set $N_{\bullet}(\CC)$, where $N_k(\CC)$ is the set of all $k$-tuples of composable morphisms 
    \[ A_0 \to A_1 \to \cdots \to A_k. \]
    The degeneracy map $\sigma_i : N_k(\CC) \to N_{k+1}(\CC)$ sends a $k$-tuple of composable morphisms
    \[ A_0 \to \cdots \to A_k \]
    to the $(k+1)$-tuple where we add the morphism $A_i \xto{\Id{A_i}} A_i$.

    The face map $\delta_i : N_k(\CC) \to N_{k-1}(\CC)$ removes the $i$-th object of a sequence by eventually composing morphisms. That is, it sends a $k$-tuple of composable morphisms
    \[ A_0 \to A_1 \to \cdots \to A_k \]
    to the tuple 
    \[ A_0 \to \cdots \to A_{i-1} \to A_{i+1} \to \cdots \to A_k. \]
    If $i$ is $0$ (respectively $k$), the first (resp. last) term is simply removed.
\end{definition}

Taking the nerve is a functor from the category of small categories to the category of simplicial sets.

\begin{example}[The nerve of a discrete category]
    Let $X$ be a set. Let $U(X)$ denote the discrete category whose objects are elements of $X$. Since the only morphisms of this category are identites, the nerve of $U(X)$ is given degree-wise by
    \[ N_{k}(U(X)) = \{(id_{x})_{i = 1}^{k} | x \in X \}.\]
    In particular, for $k \geq 2$, every $k$-simplex is obtained via a degeneracy map.
\end{example}


By thinking of the standard $n$-simplex as the $n$-dimensional simplex in euclidean space, each simplicial set can then be interpreted as a topological space using \cref{prop:sset_as_colimit}

\begin{definition}
    The \emph{geometric realization} is a functor
    \[ \abs{-} : \sSet \to \Top. \]
    On the standard $n$-simplices, it is defined as
    \[ \abs{\Delta^n} = \left\{\left(x_0, \ldots, x_n\right) \in \RR^{n+1} \middle| \sum_{i = 0}^{n} x_i = 1 \right\}. \]
    For an arbitrary simplicial set $X \in \Ob{\sSet}$, it is defined as the colimit
    \[ \abs{X} = \lim_{\Delta^n \to X} \abs{\Delta^{n}}. \]
\end{definition}

Among other things, this allows the construction of a functor from the category of small categories to topological spaces
\[ \abs{N_{\bullet}(-)} : \Cat \to \Top, \]
called the \emph{geometric realisation} of a category.

Intuitively, the realization of a simplicial set is understood as sending each $n$-simplex to the $n$-dimensional simplex, and glueing them along the face and degeneracy maps.

\begin{example}[Discrete categories, continued]\label{example:discrete_cat_realisation}
    Recall the nerve of the category $U(X)$ for a set $X$ is given degree-wise by $k$-fold copies of the identities. 
\end{example}

\textbf{Develop how natural transformations between functors induce homotopies between the continuous maps obtained via geometric realization}



As a direct use of the link between natural transformations and homotopies, we obtain the following result

\begin{proposition}\label{proposition:terminal_implies_contractible}
    Let $\CC$ be a category with a terminal object $*$. Then the geometric realization of $\CC$ is contractible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Consider the endofunctors $\Id{\CC} : \CC \to \CC$ and $* : \CC \to \CC$, where $*$ sends every object to $*$ and each morphism to $\Id{*}$. There is a natural transformation $\tau : \Id{\CC} \to *$ given by sending every object $C \in \Ob{\CC}$ to the unique morphism $C \to *$. This is indeed a natural transformation by the uniqueness of the morphisms $C \to *$ for a given object $C \in \Ob{\CC}$. 
    
    Consequently, this induces a homotopy between $\Id{} : \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)} \to \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)}$ and the constant map $* : \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)} \to \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)}$, which means that the space $\abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)}$ is contractible.
\end{proof}
