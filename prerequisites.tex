\subsection{Welcome to the simplicial world}

\subsubsection{Simplicial objects}
Simplices prominently arise in many aspects of algebraic topology, from simplicial complexes to singular (co-)homology. They may be regarded all together as a category which gives rise to interesting geometric structures.

\begin{definition}
    The \emph{simplex category} $\Delta$ consists of objects given by sets $\textbf{n}$ and non-strictly order-preserving maps.
    The maps of $\Delta$ are generated by \emph{coface maps} 
    \[ \delta_i : j \in \textbf{n} \mapsto 
    \begin{cases}
        j \in \textbf{n+1} & \text{if } j < i \\
        j + 1 \in \textbf{n+1} & \text{if } j \geq i
    \end{cases}
    \]
    with $0 \leq j \leq n$ and \emph{codegeneracy maps}
    \[
        \sigma_i : j \in \textbf{n+1} \mapsto \begin{cases}
            j \in \textbf{n} & \text{if } j \leq i \\
            j - 1 \in \textbf{n} & \text{if } j > i
        \end{cases}
    \]
    with $0 \leq j \leq n$.
\end{definition}

The reason for naming the generators coface and codegeneracy maps is that they will represent face and degeneracy maps between geometric simplices, i.e. projecting an $(n+1)$-simplex onto one of its faces and embedding an $n$-simplex as the face of an $(n+1)$-simplex.

\begin{definition}
    Let $\CC$ be a category. A \emph{simplicial object} in $\CC$ is a functor $C_{\bullet}: \op{\Delta} \to \CC$. We denote $C_n$ for the object $C_{\bullet}(\textbf{n})$.

    The images of codegeneracy maps $\delta_i : \textbf{n+1} \to \textbf{n}$ are called \emph{degeneracy maps} $d_i : C_{n+1} \to C_n$ and the images of coface maps $\sigma_i : \textbf{n} \to \textbf{n+1}$ are called \emph{face maps} $s_i : C_{n+1} \to C_{n}$.


    A \emph{simplicial set} is a simplicial object in the category of sets. The category of simplicial sets with natural transformations between them is denoted $\sSet$. For a simplicial set $X_{\bullet}$, the elements of $X_n$ that are in the image of a degeneracy map are called degeneracies, while those that are in the image of a face map are called faces.

    A \emph{pointed simplicial set} is a simplicial set $X_\bullet$ with a distinguished $0$-simplex $* \in X_0$
    
    A \emph{simplicial space} is a simplicial object in the category of topological spaces.
\end{definition}

Since all morphisms in $\Delta$ are generated by coface and codegeneracies maps, it is sufficient to define the functor on the codegeneracy and coface maps. That means, for a given codegeneracy $\delta_i : \textbf{n+1} \to \textbf{n}$, to define a map $d_i: C_{n+1} \to C_n$ and, for a given coface map $\sigma_i : \textbf{n} \to \textbf{n+1}$, to define a map $s_i : C_{n+1} \to C_{n}$. One must then check functoriality on the defined maps.

The simplest example of a simplicial set comes naturally from the Hom-sets of the simplex category.

\begin{example}[The standard $n$-simplex]
    The standard $n$-simplex $\Delta^n_{\bullet}$ is the simplicial set defined as the functor
    \[ \Delta^n_{\bullet} = \Hom[\Delta]{-}{\textbf{n}} : \op{\Delta} \to \Set. \]
    In particular $\Delta^0$ has a single $n$-simplex for all $n \geq 0$, namely the only map $\textbf{n} \to \textbf{0}$.
\end{example}


\begin{digression}[Pointed objects and category theory]
    Many categories inherit a notion of a pointed object: topological spaces, sets and simplicial sets, for example. There is a very categorical way to describe what a pointed object should be.

    Let $\CC$ be a category with a terminal object $*$. A \emph{pointed object} in $\CC$ is a pair $(C,c_0)$, where $C \in \Ob{\CC}$ and $c_0$ is a morphism from $*$ to $\CC$.

    In topological spaces, a terminal object is a singleton $\{*\}$, so that a pointed topological space is indeed a space $X$ with a continuous map $\{*\} \to X$, i.e., a choice of a point $x_0 \in X$. The same can be said of sets.

    For simplicial sets, a terminal object is given by the standard $0$-simplex $\Delta^0_{\bullet}$. Indeed, a morphism $X_\bullet \to \Delta^0_{\bullet}$ is the assignment of a morphism $f_n : X_n \to \Delta^0_{n}$ to each $\textbf{n} \in \Delta$ in a natural way. Since $Delta^n$ has a single simplex in each degree, the $f_n$ sends all simplices of $X_n$ to it. It is thus uniquely defined, and it necessarily gives a natural transformation. \\
    Consequently, a pointed simplicial set should correspond to a simplicial set $X_\bullet$ with a choice of a map $\Delta^0_{\bullet} \to X_\bullet$. This is indeed equivalent to the definition given before: choosing a point $x_0 \in X_0$ results in a map $\Delta^0_{\bullet} \to X_\bullet$ by sending the only simplex of $\Delta^0_n$ to ${s_0}^{n}(x_0) \in X_n$. Likewise, a map $\Delta^0_{\bullet} \to X_{\bullet}$ gives as basepoint the image of the only simplex in $\Delta^0_0$.
\end{digression}

Standard simplices are an essential tool to understand general simplicial sets and will be necessary for geometric interpretation.

\begin{proposition}[Lemma~2.1 in \cite{Goerss2009}]\label{prop:sset_as_colimit}
    Let $X_{\bullet}$ be a simplicial set. There is a bijection
    \[ X_n \cong \Hom[\sSet]{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}{X_{\bullet}}. \]
    Furthermore, the simplicial set $X$ is obtained via the colimit
    \[ X_{\bullet} \cong \colim_{\Delta^n_{\bullet} \to X_{\bullet}} \Delta^n \]
    taken in the category of simplices $\Delta \downarrow X_{\bullet}$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Let $\textbf{n} \in \Delta$. By the Yoneda lemma, there is a bijection
    \[ X_n = X_{\bullet}(\textbf{n}) \cong \Hom[\Cat]{\Hom[\op{\Delta}]{\textbf{n}}{-}}{X_{\bullet}} \cong \Hom[\Cat]{\Hom[\Delta]{-}{\textbf{n}}}{X_{\bullet}}, \]
    meaning there is a bijection $ X_n \cong \Hom[\sSet]{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}{X_{\bullet}}$.
    
    The second part is a consequence of the density theorem for presheaves. \textbf{Add more details}
\end{proof}

Every category gives rise to a simplicial set by chains of composable morphisms.

\begin{definition}
    Let $\CC$ be a small category. The \emph{nerve} of the category $\CC$ is the simplicial set $N_{\bullet}(\CC)$, where $N_k(\CC)$ is the set of all $k$-tuples of composable morphisms 
    \[ A_0 \to A_1 \to \cdots \to A_k. \]
    In degree $0$, the nerve is the set of objects of $\CC$.

    The degeneracy map $s_i : N_k(\CC) \to N_{k+1}(\CC)$ sends a $k$-tuple of composable morphisms
    \[ A_0 \to \cdots \to A_k \]
    to the $(k+1)$-tuple where we add the morphism $A_i \xto{\Id{A_i}} A_i$.

    The face map $d_i : N_k(\CC) \to N_{k-1}(\CC)$ removes the $i$-th object of a sequence by  composing morphisms. That is, it sends a $k$-tuple of composable morphisms
    \[ A_0 \to A_1 \to \cdots \to A_k \]
    to the tuple 
    \[ A_0 \to \cdots \to A_{i-1} \to A_{i+1} \to \cdots \to A_k. \]
    If $i$ is $0$ (respectively $k$), the first (resp. last) term is simply removed.
\end{definition}

The nerve construction underlies a functor from the category of small categories to the category of simplicial sets.

\begin{example}[The nerve of a discrete category]
    Let $X$ be a set. Let $U(X)$ denote the discrete category whose objects are elements of $X$. Since the only morphisms of this category are identities, the nerve of $U(X)$ is given degree-wise by
    \[ N_{k}(U(X)) = \{(id_{x})_{i = 1}^{k} | x \in X \}.\]
    In particular, for $k \geq 1$, every $k$-simplex is a degeneracy.
\end{example}


\subsubsection{Geometric realizations}

By thinking of the standard $n$-simplex as the $n$-dimensional simplex in Euclidean space, each simplicial set can then be interpreted as a topological space using \cref{prop:sset_as_colimit}

\begin{definition}
    \emph{Geometric realization} is a functor
    \[ \abs{-} : \sSet \to \Top \]
    that is defined on the standard $n$-simplices as
    \[ \abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}} = \left\{\left(x_0, \ldots, x_n\right) \in \RR^{n+1} \middle| \sum_{i = 0}^{n} x_i = 1 \right\}. \]
    For an arbitrary simplicial set $X_\bullet \in \Ob{\sSet}$, it is defined as the colimit
    \[ \abs{X_{\bullet}} = \colim_{\Delta^n_{\bullet} \to X_\bullet} \abs{\Delta^{n}_{\bullet}}. \]
\end{definition}

Any map $\alpha : \textbf{m} \to \textbf{n}$ gives a natural map $\Delta^m_{\bullet} \to \Delta^n_{\bullet}$ by post-composition. In particular, applying then geometric realization gives a map $\alpha_* : \abs{\Delta^m_{\bullet}} \to \abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}$. For a codegeneracy map $\sigma_i : \textbf{n} \to \textbf{n+1}$, the induced map of spaces $\sigma_{i*} : \abs{\Delta^{n+1}_{\bullet}} \to \abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}$ is the projection onto the $i$-th face of $\Delta^{n+1}_{\bullet}$. Similarly, a coface map $\delta_i : \textbf{n+1} \to \textbf{n}$ gives a map $\delta_{i*} : \abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}} \to \abs{\Delta^{n+1}_{\bullet}}$ embedding the topological $n$-simplex into the $i$-th face of $\Delta^{n+1}_{\bullet}$.

Intuitively, the realization of a simplicial set is understood as sending each $n$-simplex to the $n$-dimensional simplex, and glueing them along the face and degeneracy maps. This is actually given explicitly by another way of expressing geometric realization, which we state now.

\begin{proposition}
    If $X_\bullet$ is a simplicial set, then its geometric realization is given by the quotient space
    \[ \abs{X_\bullet} \cong \bigsqcup_{p \geq 0} X_n \times \abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}} \big/ {\sim},\]
    where the equivalence relation is generated by the identifications 
    \[(x, \alpha_{*}(\xi)) \sim (X_{\bullet}(\alpha)(x), \xi) \]
    for any $x \in X_m$ and $\xi \in \abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}$, and any map $\alpha : \textbf{n} \to \textbf{m}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of proof]
    Recall that elements of $X_n$ are in bijection with natural transformations $\Delta^n_{\bullet} \to X_{\bullet}$. In particular elements of $\Delta \downarrow X_{\bullet}$ all correspond to some $x \in X_n$. In particular, the colimit $\colim_{\Delta^n_{\bullet} \to X_{\bullet}} \Delta^n_{\bullet}$ identifies $\Delta^n_{\bullet} \to X_{\bullet}$ (corresponding to $x \in X_n$) with $\Delta^m_{\bullet} \to X_{\bullet}$ (corresponding to $y \in X_m$) if there is a map $\alpha : \textbf{n} \to \textbf{m}$ such that $X_{\bullet}(\alpha)(x) = y$. This is precisely the same type of identification made by the equivalence relation $(x, \alpha_{*}(\xi)) \sim (X_{\bullet}(\alpha)(x), \xi)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    This alternate construction allows us in particular to see that degenerate simplices add nothing to the topological space. Indeed, let $x \in X_n$ be a degenerate $n$-simplex, i.e., there exists $y \in X_{n-1}$ such that $s_i(y) = x$ for some degeneracy map. The identification
    \[ (x, \xi) = (s_i (y), \xi) \sim (y, \sigma_{i *}(\xi)),\]
    implies that $(x, \xi)$ is equivalent to a point coming from $X_{n-1}$ for any $\xi \in \abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}$.
\end{remark}

Studying maps between the realizations of the standard simplices and topological spaces naturally leads us back to the category of simplices, yielding an adjunction.

\begin{propdef}
    There is a functor $S : \Top \to \sSet$ sending a topological space $T$ to the simplicial set $S(T)$, called the \emph{singular set} of $T$, given degree-wise by
    \[ (S(T))_n = \Hom[\Top]{\abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}}{T}. \]
    Furthermore, there is an adjunction
    \[ \abs{ - } \dashv S(-). \]
\end{propdef}

\begin{proof}
    To see $S(T)$ is a simplicial set, notice that a map $f : \textbf{n} \to \textbf{m}$ induces a map $f: \Delta^m_{\bullet} \to \Delta^n_{\bullet}$ given by pre-composition. Functoriality of geometric realization then yields a continuous map $f : \abs{\Delta^m_{\bullet}} \to \abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}$. Using pre-composition, this gives a map
    \[ (S(T))_m = \Hom[\Top]{\abs{\Delta^m_{\bullet}}}{T} \to \Hom[\Top]{\abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}}{T} = (S(T))_n . \]

    To establish the adjunction, observe that

    \begin{align*}
    \Hom[\Top]{\abs{X_{\bullet}}}{T} &\overset{(1)}{\cong} \lim_{\Delta^n_{\bullet} \to X_{\bullet}} \Hom[\Top]{\abs{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}}{T} \\
    &\overset{(2)}{\cong} \lim_{\Delta^n_{\bullet} \to X_{\bullet}} \Hom[\sSet]{\Delta^n_{\bullet}}{S(T)} \\
    &\cong \Hom[\sSet]{X_{\bullet}}{S(T)}.
    \end{align*}

    The first bijection amounts to the contravariant Hom-functor sending colimits to limits.
    
    \textbf{Develop second bijection}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    Since geometric realization is a left adjoint, it preserves colimits.
\end{remark}


Compositing nerve and geometric realization gives rise to a functor from the category of small categories to topological spaces
\[ \abs{N_{\bullet}(-)} : \Cat \to \Top, \]
called the \emph{geometric realization} or \emph{classifying space} of a category.

Many of the fundamental topological spaces may be modelled as simplicial sets, allowing for a more combinatorial approach to topology. 

\begin{example}[The interval category]\label{example:interval}
    Consider the interval category $\{0 \to 1 \}$. Its nerve in degree $n$ is given by $n$-tuples of composable morphisms. Since there is only a single non-identity morphism, this is given by sequences of $k$ copies of $\Id{0}$, one copy of $0 \to 1$ (if $k$ is neither $0$ nor $n$), and $n - k$ copies of $\Id{1}$. In particular, every simplex in degree greater or equal to $2$ is degenerate, and the only non-degenerate simplex in degree $1$ is the morphism $0 \to 1$ linking the $0$-simplices $0$ and $1$. The geometric realization $\catreal{\{0 \to 1 \}}$ is thus obtained by glueing two $0$-dimensional simplices (i.e. two points) by a single $1$-dimensional simplex, yielding a closed interval homeomorphic to $[0,1]$.
\end{example}

Just as functors between categories become continuous maps between their realizations, natural transformations directly translate geometrically as well.

\begin{proposition}
    If $\tau: F \to G$ is a natural transformation between the functors $F : \CC \to \DD$ and $G : \CC \to \DD$, then $\tau$ induces a homotopy between the induced continuous maps $f : \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)} \to \abs{N_{\bullet}(\DD)}$ and $g : \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)} \to \abs{N_{\bullet}(\DD)}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    A natural transformation may be seen as a functor
    \[ \tau: \CC \times \{0 \to 1 \} \to \DD. \]
    Indeed, we may define $\tau(C, 0) = F(C)$ and $\tau(C, 1) = G(C)$ as its effect on objects, and $\tau(f, 0 \to 1) = \tau_D \circ F(f) = G(f) \circ \tau_C$. It therefore induces a continuous map
    \[ \catreal{\tau} : \catreal{\CC \times \{0 \to 1\}} \to \catreal{\DD}. \]
    Since geometric realization preserves products (\textbf{need to show}) and using \cref{example:interval}, 
    \[ \catreal{\CC \times \{0 \to 1\}} \cong \catreal{\CC} \times \catreal{\{0 \to 1\}} \cong \catreal{\CC} \times I, \]
    so $\catreal{\tau}$ can be seen as a homotopy. By the computations done in the example, we indeed obtain that $\catreal{\tau}(x,0) = F(x)$ and $\catreal{\tau}(x,1) = G(x)$.
\end{proof}

As a direct use of the link between natural transformations and homotopies, we obtain the following useful result.

\begin{proposition}\label{proposition:terminal_implies_contractible}
    If $\CC$ is a category with an initial or terminal object $*$, then the geometric realization of $\CC$ is contractible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    We consider the case where $\CC$ has a terminal object. Consider the endofunctors $\Id{\CC} : \CC \to \CC$ and $* : \CC \to \CC$, where $*$ sends every object to $*$ and each morphism to $\Id{*}$. There is a natural transformation $\tau : \Id{\CC} \to *$ given by sending every object $C \in \Ob{\CC}$ to the unique morphism $C \to *$. This is indeed a natural transformation by the uniqueness of the morphisms $C \to *$ for a given object $C \in \Ob{\CC}$. 
    
    Consequently, $\tau$ induces a homotopy between $\Id{} : \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)} \to \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)}$ and the constant map $* : \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)} \to \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)}$, which means that the space $\abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)}$ is contractible.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Simplicial objects over simplicial sets}
Since simplicial sets form a category, we may naturally consider functors from $\op{\Delta}$ to $\sSet$ as a special kind of simplicial objects.

\begin{propdef}
    A \emph{bisimplicial set} is equivalently a simplicial object over $\sSet$, i.e. a functor
    \[ (X_\bullet)_\bullet : \op{\Delta} \to \sSet, \]
    or a functor
    \[ X_{\bullet, \bullet} : \op{\Delta} \times \op{\Delta} \to \Set. \]
\end{propdef}
\begin{proof}
Recall there is a natural isomorphism of functor categories 
\[ \Hom[\Cat]{\CC}{\Hom[\Cat]{\DD}{\mathcal{E}}} \cong \Hom[\Cat]{\CC \times \DD}{\mathcal{E}} \]
given by evaluation, as long as both $\CC$ and $\DD$ are small. Consequently, there is a natural isomorphism
\[ \Hom[\Cat]{\op{\Delta}}{\Hom[\Cat]{\op{\Delta}}{\Set}} \cong \Hom[\Cat]{\op{\Delta} \times \op{\Delta}}{\Set}, \]
proving both definitions are equivalent.
\end{proof}

Naturally, the construction may be iterated to obtain \emph{multisimplicial sets}.
