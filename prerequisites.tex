\section{Just enough category theory to be dangerous}
As this thesis strongly relies on many notions in category theory, we present here some tools that will be used throughout.

\subsection{Simplicial objects and geometric realizations}
Simplices prominently arise in many aspects of algebraic topology, such as simplicial complexes to singular (co-)homology. They may be regarded all together as a category which gives rise to interesting geometric structures.

\begin{definition}
    The \emph{simplex category} $\Delta$ consists of objects given by sets $\textbf{n}$ and non-strictly order-preserving maps.
    The maps of $\Delta$ are generated by \emph{coface maps} 
    \[ \delta_i : j \in \textbf{n} \mapsto 
    \begin{cases}
        j \in \textbf{n+1} & \text{if } j < i \\
        j + 1 \in \textbf{n+1} & \text{if } j \geq i
    \end{cases}
    \]
    with $0 \leq j \leq n$ and \emph{codegeneracy maps}
    \[
        \sigma_i : j \in \textbf{n+1} \mapsto \begin{cases}
            j \in \textbf{n} & \text{if } j \leq i \\
            j - 1 \in \textbf{n} & \text{if } j > i
        \end{cases}
    \]
    with $0 \leq j \leq n$.
\end{definition}

The reason for naming the generators coface and codegeneracy maps is that they will represent face and degeneracy maps between geometric simplices, i.e. projecting an $(n+1)$-simplex onto one of its faces and embedding an $n$-simplex as the face of an $(n+1)$-simplex.

\begin{definition}
    Let $\CC$ be a category. A \emph{simplicial object} in $\CC$ is a functor $S: \op{\Delta} \to \CC$. We denote $C_n$ for the object $S(\textbf{n})$.

    The images of codegeneracy maps $\delta_i : \textbf{n+1} \to \textbf{n}$ are called \emph{degeneracy maps} $d_i : C_{n+1} \to C_n$ and the images of coface maps $\sigma_i : \textbf{n} \to \textbf{n+1}$ are called \emph{face maps} $s_i : C_{n+1} \to C_{n}$.


    A \emph{simplicial set} is a simplicial object in the category of sets. The category of simplicial sets with natural transformations between them is denoted $\sSet$. For a simplicial set $X$, the elements of $X_n$ that are in the image of a degeneracy map are called degeneracies, while those that are in the image of a face map are called faces.
    
    A \emph{simplicial space} is a simplicial object in the category of topological spaces.
\end{definition}

Since all morphisms in $\Delta$ are generated by coface and codegeneracies maps, it is sufficient to define the functor on the codegeneracy and coface maps. That means, for a given codegeneracy $\delta_i : \textbf{n+1} \to \textbf{n}$, to define a map $d_i: C_{n+1} \to C_n$ and, for a given coface map $\sigma_i : \textbf{n} \to \textbf{n+1}$, to define a map $s_i : C_{n+1} \to C_{n}$. One must then check functoriality on the defined maps.

The simplest example of a simplicial set comes naturally from the Hom-sets of the simplex category.

\begin{example}[The standard $n$-simplex]
    The standard $n$-simplex $\Delta^n$ is the simplicial set defined as the functor
    \[ \Hom[\Delta]{-}{\textbf{n}} : \op{\Delta} \to \Set. \]
\end{example}

Every category gives rise to a simplicial set by chains of composable morphisms.

\begin{definition}
    Let $\CC$ be a small category. The \emph{nerve} of the category $\CC$ is the simplicial set $N_{\bullet}(\CC)$, where $N_k(\CC)$ is the set of all $k$-tuples of composable morphisms 
    \[ A_0 \to A_1 \to \cdots \to A_k. \]
    In degree $0$, the nerve is the set of objects of $\CC$.

    The degeneracy map $\sigma_i : N_k(\CC) \to N_{k+1}(\CC)$ sends a $k$-tuple of composable morphisms
    \[ A_0 \to \cdots \to A_k \]
    to the $(k+1)$-tuple where we add the morphism $A_i \xto{\Id{A_i}} A_i$.

    The face map $\delta_i : N_k(\CC) \to N_{k-1}(\CC)$ removes the $i$-th object of a sequence by eventually composing morphisms. That is, it sends a $k$-tuple of composable morphisms
    \[ A_0 \to A_1 \to \cdots \to A_k \]
    to the tuple 
    \[ A_0 \to \cdots \to A_{i-1} \to A_{i+1} \to \cdots \to A_k. \]
    If $i$ is $0$ (respectively $k$), the first (resp. last) term is simply removed.
\end{definition}

Taking the nerve is a functor from the category of small categories to the category of simplicial sets.

\begin{example}[The nerve of a discrete category]
    Let $X$ be a set. Let $U(X)$ denote the discrete category whose objects are elements of $X$. Since the only morphisms of this category are identities, the nerve of $U(X)$ is given degree-wise by
    \[ N_{k}(U(X)) = \{(id_{x})_{i = 1}^{k} | x \in X \}.\]
    In particular, for $k \geq 2$, every $k$-simplex is obtained via a degeneracy map.
\end{example}


By thinking of the standard $n$-simplex as the $n$-dimensional simplex in Euclidean space, each simplicial set can then be interpreted as a topological space using \cref{prop:sset_as_colimit}

\begin{definition}
    The \emph{geometric realization} is a functor
    \[ \abs{-} : \sSet \to \Top. \]
    On the standard $n$-simplices, it is defined as
    \[ \abs{\Delta^n} = \left\{\left(x_0, \ldots, x_n\right) \in \RR^{n+1} \middle| \sum_{i = 0}^{n} x_i = 1 \right\}. \]
    For an arbitrary simplicial set $X \in \Ob{\sSet}$, it is defined as the colimit
    \[ \abs{X} = \lim_{\Delta^n \to X} \abs{\Delta^{n}}. \]
\end{definition}

Intuitively, the realization of a simplicial set is understood as sending each $n$-simplex to the $n$-dimensional simplex, and glueing them along the face and degeneracy maps.

Studying maps between the realizations of the standard simplices and topological spaces naturally returns to the category of simplices, and yields an adjunction.

\begin{propdef}
    There is a functor $S : \Top \to \sSet$ sending a topological space $T$ to the simplicial set $S(T)$, called the \emph{singular set} of $T$, given degree-wise by
    \[ (S(T))_n = \Hom[\Top]{\abs{\Delta^n}}{T}. \]
    Furthermore, there is an adjunction
    \[ \abs{ - } \dashv S(-) \]
\end{propdef}

\begin{proof}
    To see $S(T)$ is a simplicial set, notice a map $f : \textbf{n} \to \textbf{m}$ induces a map $f: \Delta^m \to \Delta^n$ given by pre-composition. Functoriality of geometric realization then yields a continuous map $f : \abs{\Delta^m} \to \abs{\Delta^n}$. Using pre-composition, this gives a map
    \[ (S(T))_m = \Hom[\Top]{\abs{\Delta^m}}{T} \to \Hom[\Top]{\abs{\Delta^n}}{T} = (S(T))_n . \]

    To show the adjunction, notice that

    \begin{align*}
    \Hom[\Top]{\abs{X}}{T} &\overset{(1)}{\cong} \lim_{\Delta^n \to X} \Hom[\Top]{\abs{\Delta^n}}{T} \\
    &\overset{(2)}{\cong} \lim_{\Delta^n \to X} \Hom[\sSet]{\Delta^n}{S(T)} \\
    &\cong \Hom[\sSet]{X}{S(T)}.
    \end{align*}

    The first bijection amounts to the contravariant Hom-functor sends colimits to limits.
    \textbf{Develop second bijection}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    Since geometric realization is a left adjoint, it preserves colimits.

    Furthermore, there is a functor from simplicial sets to simplicial groups taking a simplicial set $X$ to the simplicial group $\ZZ X$ given degree-wise by $\ZZ X_n$. In this way, the homology groups of $\ZZ S(T)$ are precisely the singular homology groups of $T$.
\end{remark}


Using both nerve and geometric realization allows for the construction of a functor from the category of small categories to topological spaces
\[ \abs{N_{\bullet}(-)} : \Cat \to \Top, \]
called the \emph{geometric realization} of a category.

\begin{example}{The interval category}\label{example:interval}
    Consider the interval category $\{0 \to 1 \}$. Its nerve in degree $n$ is given by $n$-tuples of composable morphisms. Since there is only a single non-identity morphism, this is given by sequences of $k$ copies of $\Id{0}$, one copy of $o \to 1$ (if $k$ is neither $0$ nor $n$), and $n - k$ copies of $\Id{1}$. In particular, every simplex in degree greater or equal to $2$ is degenerate, and the only non-degenerate simplex in degree $1$ is the morphism $0 \to 1$ linking the $0$-simplices $0$ and $1$. The geometric realization $\catreal{\{0 \to 1 \}}$ is thus obtained by glueing two $0$-dimensional simplices (i.e. two points) by a single $1$-dimensional simplex, yielding a closed interval $[0,1]$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Discrete categories, continued]\label{example:discrete_cat_realisation}
    Recall the nerve of the category $U(X)$ for a set $X$ is given degree-wise by $k$-fold copies of the identities. In particular, every simplex in degree greater or equal to $1$ is degenerate. As a consequence, $\catreal{U(X)}$ may be obtained as the disjoint union of its $0$-simplices, meaning it is precisely the set $X$ with the discrete topology.
\end{example}

Just like functors between categories become continuous maps between their realizations, natural transformations directly translate geometrically as well.

\begin{proposition}
    Let $\tau: F \to G$ be a natural transformation between the functors $F : \CC \to \DD$ and $G : \CC \to \DD$. Then $\tau$ induces a homotopy between the induced continuous maps $f : \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)} \to \abs{N_{\bullet}(\DD)}$ and $g : \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)} \to \abs{N_{\bullet}(\DD)}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    A natural transformation may be seen as a functor
    \[ \tau: \CC \times \{0 \to 1 \} \to \DD. \]
    Indeed, we may define $\tau(C, 0) = F(C)$ and $\tau(C, 1) = G(C)$ as its effect on objects, and $\tau(f, 0 \to 1) = \tau_D \circ F(f) = G(f) \circ \tau_C$. It therefore induces a continuous map
    \[ \catreal{\tau} : \catreal{\CC \times \{0 \to 1\}} \to \catreal{\DD}. \]
    Since geometric realization preserves products (\textbf{need to show}) and using \cref{example:interval}, the domain is actually
    \[ \catreal{\CC \times \{0 \to 1\}} \cong \catreal{\CC} \times \catreal{\{0 \to 1\}} \cong \catreal{\CC} \times I, \]
    so $\catreal{\tau}$ is a homotopy. By the computations done in the example, we indeed obtain that $\catreal{\tau}(x,0) = F(x)$ and $\catreal{\tau}(x,1) = G(x)$.
\end{proof}

As a direct use of the link between natural transformations and homotopies, we obtain the following result

\begin{proposition}\label{proposition:terminal_implies_contractible}
    Let $\CC$ be a category with an initial or terminal object $*$. Then the geometric realization of $\CC$ is contractible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    We consider the case where $\CC$ has a terminal object. Consider the endofunctors $\Id{\CC} : \CC \to \CC$ and $* : \CC \to \CC$, where $*$ sends every object to $*$ and each morphism to $\Id{*}$. There is a natural transformation $\tau : \Id{\CC} \to *$ given by sending every object $C \in \Ob{\CC}$ to the unique morphism $C \to *$. This is indeed a natural transformation by the uniqueness of the morphisms $C \to *$ for a given object $C \in \Ob{\CC}$. 
    
    Consequently, this induces a homotopy between $\Id{} : \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)} \to \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)}$ and the constant map $* : \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)} \to \abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)}$, which means that the space $\abs{N_{\bullet}(\CC)}$ is contractible.
\end{proof}
